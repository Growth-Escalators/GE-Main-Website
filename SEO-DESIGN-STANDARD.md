# Growth Escalators SEO & Commercial Design Standard

Updated: 24 Aug 2026

This document is the operating standard for commercial/SEO landing pages on growthescalators.com. The `/d2c` page is the reference for design language, information hierarchy, conversion philosophy, SEO architecture, AI-search semantics, visual density and commercial decision logic. It is **not** a universal section-by-section template.

## 1. Priority order

1. SEO preservation and search intent
2. Commercial clarity and proof
3. Conversion usefulness
4. Design and motion

Design must never remove crawlable topic coverage, internal links, metadata/schema parity, useful FAQ content or existing indexed copy merely to make a page shorter.

## 2. Before changing an existing page

Freeze and review:

- primary and secondary keyword intent
- commercial/local/industry intent
- supporting long-tail questions
- current title, meta description, canonical and H1
- current schema and visible facts
- current FAQ questions
- inbound and outbound internal links
- related category/service/blog resources
- useful indexed copy and proof that should be retained

Do not change a URL, canonical, title/H1 target, high-value FAQ, schema entity or important internal-link relationship casually. When a change is necessary, the PR must state why.

## 3. Page jobs

Every major section should have one primary job:

- **DIAGNOSE** — show the commercial/search problem
- **EXPLAIN** — make the system/economics understandable
- **PROVE** — provide attributable evidence
- **CONVERT** — give a specific useful next step

Merge neighbouring sections that repeat the same job. Avoid repeated “process / framework / why us / how we work” sections that say the same thing.

## 4. Buyer-specific story, shared GE design language

Pages should share the Growth Escalators visual system without sharing one rigid sequence.

Examples:

- Healthcare: search demand → local visibility → treatment/appointment economics → trust → qualification/follow-up → proof → capabilities → FAQ
- Real estate: project economics → lead quality → qualification → site visits → sales handoff → inventory/launch phase → proof → capabilities
- White-label: partner capacity problem → client protection → operating model/SLA → one-client pilot → fulfilment capabilities → proof → engagement model
- Software: business requirement → architecture → build approach → technology → proof → delivery model → cost/timeline questions

## 5. Design language

Target feeling: premium, commercial, analytical, modern, confident and system-oriented.

Use:

- editorial typography for major statements
- SaaS/dashboard-style system UI when it explains economics
- consulting-quality information architecture
- real commercial proof
- deep navy/purple, white/warm neutrals, soft lavender/blue and GE orange as an accent
- soft mint/green only for positive-data emphasis when useful

Avoid:

- generic 3-card service grids everywhere
- decorative gradient blobs without information value
- emoji icons as the primary design language
- generic stock photography
- repetitive rounded white cards
- excessive glassmorphism
- meaningless animation
- large empty scroll areas
- generic “Why Choose Us?” filler

Use three visual modes across a long page:

1. Light / editorial
2. System / data
3. High-emphasis dark/commercial proof or CTA

## 6. Typography and spacing

Use three levels:

1. Major editorial statements — only a few per page
2. Section headings — strong but calmer
3. UI/commercial detail — metrics, labels, cards and explanations

Body copy must remain comfortably readable; do not hide important information in tiny grey text.

Typical spacing guidance:

- major section: ~72–88px desktop
- normal section: ~56–72px
- compact support: ~40–56px
- mobile: usually ~48–64px

Do not force equal card heights or 120px+ padding everywhere. Aim for approximately 25–35% less perceived scrolling than a conventional agency landing page with the same content depth.

## 7. Business system vs operating method

Do not confuse what drives the buyer's business with how Growth Escalators improves it.

Example D2C business system:

**Acquire → Convert → Monetize → Retain → Scale**

GE Six Sigma-inspired operating method:

**Define → Measure → Analyze → Improve → Control → Scale**

Define–Control is the standard five-phase DMAIC core. **Scale** is Growth Escalators' commercial extension, so volume increases after the improvement is proven and controlled. Never describe traditional DMAIC as six phases.

## 8. Commercial decision logic

Use explicit decision relationships where useful:

**IF** condition is healthy  
**BUT** another metric/step is weak  
**THEN** prioritize the intervention that fixes the constraint before adding more spend.

Example healthcare:

IF traffic volume is adequate  
BUT lead-to-appointment conversion is weak  
THEN fix qualification and follow-up before buying more traffic.

This is both strong conversion content and strong AI-search semantics.

## 9. Capabilities

Do not dump every service under “Our Services.” Prefer a constraint-led frame such as:

> The lever depends on the constraint.

The page should communicate that GE deploys the capabilities the economics indicate need fixing rather than selling every service automatically.

## 10. Proof

Prefer:

**Before → What changed → After**

Use real metrics and real project/client assets when available: dashboards, analytics, website before/after, landing-page comparisons, creative examples or verified screenshots.

Never fabricate a number, review, rating, client relationship or niche case study. Adjacent-category proof must be labelled honestly.

## 11. Images and performance

Images must earn their space by communicating proof, context, results, creative work, product/site quality or system UI. Prefer a strong information/data component over a generic filler image.

Optimize images and LCP. Do not make premium design depend on large JS bundles or background video. Prefer server-rendered HTML, CSS, SVG and lightweight components.

## 12. SEO/AEO content

Pages should make it easy for search engines and AI systems to answer:

- who GE is for
- what commercial problem the page solves
- what metrics matter
- how GE decides what to fix
- what the framework means
- what proof exists
- what services/capabilities are relevant
- what should happen when condition X occurs

Write self-contained, extractable statements. Prefer explicit relationships over vague phrases such as “data-driven” or “holistic.”

Use one H1, logical H2/H3 hierarchy and natural keyword/entity coverage. Visual font size does not determine HTML heading level.

## 13. Internal linking and topic clusters

Every important commercial page must have meaningful parent ↔ child/sibling relationships and supporting resources. Use descriptive anchors, never “click here.”

Near the lower part of a page, resource clusters may mix larger category/playbook links with smaller tactical resources instead of a wall of identical cards.

## 14. Schema

Inspect existing schema before changing it. Use only relevant types such as Organization, ProfessionalService, Service, BreadcrumbList and FAQPage where appropriate.

Structured data must match visible content. Never add fabricated reviews/ratings or stale business details.

## 15. Local SEO / NAP

Current Growth Escalators office address:

**Office No: 607-608, 6th Floor, Class of Pearl, Income Tax Colony, Durgapura, Jaipur, Rajasthan 302018, India**

Do not use the previous Sector 26 / Pratap Nagar / 302033 address.

Local pages should establish service + industry + location + commercial problem naturally, not by stuffing “Jaipur” into every heading.

## 16. Forms and conversion

Avoid generic “Contact Us” as the main proposition. Offer a concrete commercial output such as:

- Get My D2C Growth Audit
- Get My SEO Opportunity Audit
- Get My Website Conversion Audit
- Get My Lead Generation Diagnosis
- Get My Project Launch Plan
- Send One Client Requirement

Explain what the prospect receives: likely constraint, what we would investigate first, where value may be leaking and priority actions.

Forms should be buyer-specific. Healthcare asks clinic/speciality questions; white-label asks agency/client requirement questions; travel asks destination/package context; real estate asks project/launch context where useful.

## 17. Mobile and accessibility

Design mobile intentionally rather than merely stacking desktop cards. Check headline wrapping, density, button widths, form fields, FAQ touch targets, horizontal overflow, sticky navigation/CTA behaviour and card height.

Maintain semantic headings, keyboard controls, visible focus states, sufficient contrast, aria labels where appropriate and proper link/button semantics.

## 18. Rollback and deployment

Before a major redesign, create a branch/rollback point.

Before production merge:

1. audit existing SEO
2. preserve rollback point
3. implement page-specific composition
4. run SEO-integrity checks
5. run production build / TypeScript checks
6. lint where supported
7. verify static generation
8. verify preview deployment
9. verify route 200
10. verify canonical and robots/index state
11. verify metadata and schema
12. verify internal links
13. verify FAQs remain in rendered HTML
14. verify mobile layout and representative Lighthouse/CWV
15. merge only after the exact head is verified
16. verify the production deployment again after merge
17. update the SEO Preservation Register and sitemap `lastModified` only for pages whose crawlable content actually changed

## 19. Final quality test

Before considering a page complete, ask:

- Does each viewport/section add new information?
- Can the visitor understand positioning within 10 seconds?
- Can they understand how GE thinks, not just what GE sells?
- Is the proof real and sufficiently clear?
- Is whitespace improving readability rather than lengthening the page?
- Does the page answer important search questions?
- Could ChatGPT/Gemini/Perplexity extract concise answers?
- Are the right internal links present?
- Is the CTA commercially specific?
- Does mobile feel intentionally designed?

The desired final impression is:

> Growth Escalators understands the economics and operating system behind this business.

Not:

> Growth Escalators is another agency selling Meta Ads, SEO, websites and social media.
